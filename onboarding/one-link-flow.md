# The One-Link Flow

The entire onboarding philosophy: **the storyteller clicks one link and talks.** No app. No account. No password. If any step asks the elder to install, sign up, or remember something, the design failed — fix the design, don't train the elder.

## The weekly loop

1. **Monday — the archivist sends the question.** A family member (or staff) picks one prompt from the question library and sends it using the email template below. The email contains exactly one link.
2. **The storyteller clicks the link.** It opens a chat, already addressed to them by name, with the week's question waiting. They type or tap the microphone and talk. Twenty-plus languages supported.
3. **The story saves automatically** to the family's encrypted vault, tagged with the question and date.
4. **Friday — the echo.** The archivist gets a short notification: "Mom answered this week's question." Families who hear the echo keep participating. Families who don't, drift.

## Staff script (for the activities director or champion)

> "We're starting something I think you're going to love. Once a week, your family sends you a question — about your childhood, your work, your life — and you just tap the link and talk. Your stories get saved for your grandkids. You can skip any question, stop any time, and everything you record belongs to you. Want to try the first one together right now?"

Key lines to never skip: *you can skip anything, stop any time, it all belongs to you.*

## Family email template

> **Subject:** A question for [Name] this week
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> This week's question for your story collection:
>
> **[Question]**
>
> Just tap this link and talk (or type) — no app, no account:
> [ONE LINK]
>
> Your stories are saved privately for the family. Skip any question you like.
>
> — [Archivist name]

## What can go wrong

- **"I don't have anything interesting to say."** Start with a joy prompt (#26–30), not a wisdom prompt. Everyone can answer what meal they still think about.
- **Family enthusiasm fades by week three.** The Friday echo matters more than the Monday question. No echo, no habit.
- **Staff try to "help" by hovering.** Train the one line: "It's all yours — I'll be right over here if the link acts up."
- **Someone records something painful.** That's allowed. The consent template covers it: the elder decides what stays, what goes, and who hears it.
